Mortgage Options

Choose the file type first.

Most mortgage conversations become easier once the agent can identify the client’s situation. Use the list below to route clients to the right solution page and collect the right context before submitting a file.

First Time Home Buyer

For buyers entering the market for the first time. Confirm down payment source, income stability, credit history, purchase budget, and whether they may qualify for first-time buyer programs or rebates.

Second Home or Next Home

For clients moving up, downsizing, or buying a vacation/secondary property. Review sale timing, bridge financing needs, porting options, and total carrying costs.

Refinance

For homeowners replacing an existing mortgage to access equity, consolidate debt, change lenders, or improve cash flow. Check current balance, maturity date, penalty estimate, property value, and debt payout list.

Renovation Finance

For homeowners or buyers funding repairs and upgrades. Confirm renovation budget, contractor estimates, equity position, and whether a refinance, HELOC, second mortgage, or purchase-plus-improvement route fits best.

Mortgage Renewal

For clients approaching maturity. Ask for the renewal offer, current lender, remaining balance, maturity date, payment comfort, and goals for the next term.

Equity Takeout

For clients who want cash from home equity without necessarily selling. Clarify use of funds, current mortgage details, property value, credit profile, and whether repayment is temporary or long term.

Home Equity Line of Credit

For flexible access to equity over time. Useful for ongoing projects, emergency liquidity, investment opportunities, or staged expenses.

Debt Consolidation

For clients carrying high-interest credit cards, loans, or lines of credit. Collect balances, monthly payments, interest rates, and payout statements where available.

Reverse Mortgage

For older homeowners who want to access equity while staying in the property. Confirm age, ownership, property type, current mortgage balance, and long-term plans.

New to Canada Mortgage

For newcomers with limited Canadian credit history. Review residency status, employment, down payment source, international credit references, and bank statements.

Construction Financing

For custom builds, major construction, and staged funding. Collect land details, permits, budget, builder information, draw schedule, and appraisal expectations.

Commercial Mortgages

For owner-occupied or income-producing commercial property. Review business financials, lease income, property type, environmental needs, and down payment strength.

Purchase Plus Improvements

For buyers purchasing a home that needs upgrades right away. Confirm accepted purchase price, improvement quote, down payment, lender rules, and completion timing.

Co-Equity Homeownership

For buyers exploring shared-equity support to improve affordability. Review eligibility, contribution expectations, future sale rules, and long-term ownership goals.

Rate Structure

Fixed vs. variable.

Fixed rate

The interest rate stays the same for the full term. This is easier for clients who want predictable payments and budget certainty.

  • Best for stable monthly planning.
  • Common choice for first-time buyers and risk-sensitive clients.
  • Breaking early can have a larger penalty because of interest rate differential calculations.

Variable rate

The rate changes when the lender’s prime rate changes. It can save money if rates fall, but payments or interest allocation may move.

  • Best for clients comfortable with rate movement.
  • Often has a simpler three-month-interest style penalty.
  • Agent should explain payment stress and trigger-rate risk where applicable.

Payment Flexibility

Open vs. closed.

A mortgage is usually either open or closed. This affects how much the client can repay early without a penalty.

Open mortgage
Allows full repayment at any time with no prepayment charge. Rates are usually higher. Useful when the client expects to sell, receive a payout, or repay quickly.
Closed mortgage
Usually has a lower rate, but limits extra payments. Most clients choose closed terms because they plan to hold the mortgage for the term.

Agent Workflow

Simple file flow.

  1. Identify the scenario. Purchase, refinance, renewal, equity, construction, commercial, or special program.
  2. Confirm the numbers. Income, debts, credit, down payment, property value, mortgage balance, and payment comfort.
  3. Collect documents early. Missing documents slow down underwriting more than rate shopping does.
  4. Match the lender type. Bank, credit union, monoline, alternative lender, or private option depending on strength of file.
  5. Explain the trade-offs. Rate, penalty, fees, term, prepayment privileges, speed, and approval conditions.
  6. Keep the client updated. Tell them what is submitted, what is pending, and what must happen before closing.

Documents

What to ask for first.

  • Government-issued photo ID.
  • Recent pay stubs, employment letter, T4s, NOAs, or business financials if self-employed.
  • Proof of down payment or equity source, including bank statements, gift letter, or sale agreement.
  • Current mortgage statement and renewal offer for refinance or renewal files.
  • Property details, accepted offer, MLS listing, appraisal, tax bill, or lease details where applicable.
  • Debt list with balances, monthly payments, and payout statements for consolidation files.

Quick Notes

Common reminders for agents.

Do not quote final approval from a quick estimate. Always confirm lender guidelines, insurer rules, property type, and documents before presenting certainty.

Penalty matters. For refinance and early switch files, estimate the prepayment charge before comparing savings.

Property use matters. Owner-occupied, rental, vacation, commercial, and construction files are assessed differently.

Down payment source matters. Lenders need a clean paper trail for savings, gifts, sale proceeds, borrowed funds, or equity.
